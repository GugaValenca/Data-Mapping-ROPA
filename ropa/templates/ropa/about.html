About this project

What this is

Data-Mapping-ROPA is a database-backed platform for maintaining a Record of Processing Activities (ROPA) — the data map that GDPR Article 30 ("Records of processing activities") and LGPD Article 37 (the equivalent obligation to keep a record of personal data processing operations) require organizations to keep current. Instead of a static spreadsheet or Word document, processing activities are stored as structured, related records — data categories, legal bases, recipients, and retention periods are all reusable building blocks — and the ROPA itself, along with its PDF/CSV exports, is generated automatically from that data on demand.

GDPR Art. 30 and LGPD Art. 37 citations verified against eur-lex.europa.eu and planalto.gov.br on October 2, 2026 — for each article's general subject matter only, not a claim that this tool satisfies either article's specific content requirements.

Why it exists

The ROPA is too often treated as paperwork: assembled once for an audit and rarely touched again, hard to query when someone actually needs an answer ("which activities involve a cross-border transfer?", "which ones rely on consent?"). This system treats that data the way an application treats any other structured record — normalized, filterable, exportable in real time — with documentation gaps (sensitive data lacking a defined legal basis or retention period) surfacing automatically instead of waiting to be found during a review.

How it works

  • Privacy/compliance staff maintain activities through Django admin, the same way a real team would update records over time.
  • The dashboard renders the current ROPA as a filterable, searchable table — by data category, legal basis, or cross-border transfer.
  • Any activity combining a sensitive data category with an undefined legal basis or retention period is flagged automatically.
  • The full (or filtered) ROPA can be exported as a ready-to-hand-over PDF or CSV at any time.

Built with

Django · SQLite (dev) · Django templates + vanilla CSS/JS · ReportLab (PDF) · Python csv module (CSV) · 34 automated tests covering risk triage, filters, and exports

Scope & disclaimer

All company, department, and vendor names in the sample data are fictional. Legal basis labels are generic categories (Consent, Contract Necessity, Legitimate Interest, Legal Obligation), not legal advice for any specific situation. Retention periods shown are illustrative placeholders pending verification against actual law and are marked as such in the source code — they are not asserted as legally required durations.

About the author

Gustavo Valença is a Brazilian-trained lawyer with legal team leadership experience, working across Python/Django development and data privacy law (LGPD, GDPR, CCPA). This project reflects that combination directly: the data model encodes privacy-program concepts — legal bases, retention justification, cross-border transfer flags, risk triage — as a structured, tested Django application rather than as a static document.
